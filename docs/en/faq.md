??? "1. Is National Risk Watch a good fit for my National Society?"

    National Risk Watch is a good fit if your National Society wants to **monitor hazards in your country and decide in time whether to take early action or not**. It supports early warning and early action broadly: it is useful whether you work with a formal Early Action Protocol, a contingency plan, a local standard operating procedure, or no formal protocol yet.

    The platform works best when:

    - The hazards that matter to you are supported. In this pre-release that is **river floods**. More hazards are being added over time, see question 7
    - You want one shared picture of risk that you can use with your government counterparts and partners, for example on a large screen in an Emergency Operations Centre
    - You are looking for **decision support**, not a decision maker. National Risk Watch shows forecasts, alert levels and exposed population. The decision to act stays with your National Society

    National Risk Watch is a joint initiative of the Netherlands Red Cross 510 team and the IFRC GO team. It replaces the IBF Portal and is being rolled out step by step, with the ambition to support 30 National Societies by 2029. Existing IBF users are onboarded first.

    If you are unsure whether it fits your context, [contact us](mailto:support@nationalriskwatch.org) and we will look at it together.

??? "2. What are the minimum requirements for using National Risk Watch?"

    **Hardware**

    A laptop or desktop computer is enough. There is nothing to install. For use in an Emergency Operations Centre you can show the platform on a large monitor; the layout adapts to larger screens.

    **Browser and internet**

    National Risk Watch runs in a web browser, such as Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari, and needs an internet connection. It does not work offline, and there is no mobile app.

    **Access**

    In this pre-release you do not need a personal account. Open the link you were given and the platform loads. Accounts and sign in are planned for a later release. See [Logging in](./platform/logging-in.md).

    **Information from your side**

    To configure the platform for your country, we need the thresholds your National Society works with, such as the return period, probability and lead time in your Early Action Protocol, and a focal point who can check that the configuration matches your protocol.

??? "3. How much support do I need to use National Risk Watch?"

    National Risk Watch is designed to be **easy to read and easy to act on**, also for colleagues who are not forecasting specialists. Most users can find their way with a short introduction and this manual.

    The support you can expect falls into three parts:

    - **Setup and configuration:** the National Risk Watch team configures the platform for your country, including thresholds, admin areas and river stations, and checks it with your focal point
    - **Onboarding:** an introduction to the platform for your team, supported by this online manual
    - **Ongoing support:** questions and issues can be sent to your focal point or to the [National Risk Watch team](mailto:support@nationalriskwatch.org). Configuration issues, such as a threshold that does not match your protocol, can be corrected centrally

    Wider anticipatory action support, such as developing an Early Action Protocol or strengthening preparedness capacity, is provided across the network by the IFRC and Partner National Societies. We are happy to help connect you.

??? "4. How much does National Risk Watch cost me?"

    **National Risk Watch is free for National Societies.**

    The platform is centrally hosted and maintained by the Netherlands Red Cross 510 team and the IFRC GO team as one shared platform for all National Societies. You only need a computer and an internet connection to use it.

??? "5. How quickly can National Risk Watch be implemented?"

    National Societies are onboarded **as soon as the hazards that matter to them are supported** in National Risk Watch. Because the platform is centrally hosted, there is no separate instance to build for your country.

    The time needed mainly depends on configuring the platform for your country:

    - Setting the thresholds, such as return period, probability and lead time, in line with your Early Action Protocol or your agreement with your national hydrological or meteorological service
    - Loading the admin areas for your country
    - Linking the river stations to the admin areas they inform

    Your focal point then checks that the configuration matches how your National Society works. If you are moving over from the IBF Portal, the IBF Portal stays available until National Risk Watch is ready to replace it for your country. See [Coming from the IBF Portal](./release/coming-from-ibf.md).

??? "6. Can National Risk Watch be integrated with a third party tool?"

    National Risk Watch is built as part of the IFRC GO ecosystem. Besides the standalone platform described in this manual, it is also available as a view inside IFRC GO.

    Connections with other third party tools are not part of this pre-release. If your National Society or government counterparts would like to connect National Risk Watch with a tool you already use, [contact us](mailto:support@nationalriskwatch.org) so we can look at your needs and what is possible.

??? "7. When will other hazards become available?"

    This pre-release covers **river floods only**. Other hazards are being built and will be added over time:

    - **Drought** is next in scope
    - **Additional hazards**, such as tropical cyclones and epidemics, are planned from 2027 onward

    These are planned, not committed dates. The order depends on the availability of reliable data sources and on the needs of National Societies. See [What is not yet available](./release/not-yet-available.md) for the current status.

??? "8. Can we run National Risk Watch locally?"

    No. National Risk Watch is **centrally hosted and maintained as one platform for all National Societies**, and operated locally by each National Society. This keeps costs low, makes sure everyone works with the same validated data, and means improvements reach all countries at the same time.

    Full local hosting is not part of the current plan. It may be assessed on a case by case basis, together with the National Society. Offline use is not available.

??? "9. Where does the responsibility lie if the data or forecasts are incorrect?"

    **Where the data comes from**

    National Risk Watch does not produce forecasts. It brings together existing global sources and shows them on one map. For floods these are:

    - **River discharge forecasts** from GloFAS, the Global Flood Awareness System run by Copernicus
    - **Flood depth maps** from the Joint Research Centre of the European Commission
    - **Population data** from WorldPop

    Every event card lists the data sources it was built from. See [Data sources](./hazards/floods/data-sources.md).

    **Where the responsibility lies**

    - **The source data:** responsibility for the data and the forecasts lies with the providers of the source data, not with the platform. National Risk Watch aggregates and visualizes existing sources, it does not produce forecasts. Forecasts carry uncertainty and can over or under estimate in some basins. If you disagree with a forecast, the discussion is with the forecast producer and with your national services
    - **The platform:** the National Risk Watch team makes sure the data is processed and displayed as intended, checks new data sources before they are added, and configures the platform for your country
    - **The decision to act:** the responsibility to take action always lies with your National Society. National Risk Watch is a tool that can inform this decision, among other considerations such as your own protocol, local knowledge, advice from your national hydrological or meteorological service and the situation on the ground. The platform can tell you that a threshold has been crossed. It cannot tell you that acting is the right call

    If you believe a forecast, threshold or number in the platform is wrong, please [contact us](mailto:support@nationalriskwatch.org) so we can look into it.

??? "10. How can National Risk Watch be used to monitor my Early Action Protocol(s)?"

    The thresholds in your Early Action Protocol are configured in National Risk Watch for your country: the **return period**, the **probability** and the **lead time** your protocol activates on.

    - When all three conditions are met, the event card shows **trigger reached** in red, next to the alert level
    - The **advisory** on the event card reminds you which action your protocol requires
    - The **river discharge forecast** graph lets you check the forecast behind the trigger: which return period lines it crosses, on which day, and with what probability

    National Risk Watch also goes beyond Early Action Protocol monitoring. The low, medium and high alert levels show forecasts that do not reach your trigger, so you can prepare earlier. See [From early warning to early action](./introduction/early-warning-early-action.md).

    !!! Warning "Check the platform regularly"
        Alert emails and messages are not yet available in this pre-release. Check the platform as regularly as your protocol requires, and maintain your current monitoring routine.

??? "11. How can we upload local data to National Risk Watch?"

    In this pre-release you cannot upload data yourself. National Risk Watch starts with global data sources that can be checked and maintained centrally.

    Adding national and local sources, such as a national flood model or flood maps from your hydrological service, is planned. Every new source first goes through a data quality assessment, and adding it needs configuration work for your country. This makes sure that everyone can trust the data they act on.

    If your National Society, your national services or a research partner hold data that could improve the forecasts for your country, please [contact us](mailto:support@nationalriskwatch.org). Knowing what exists helps us prioritize.
